Domain-Generalized Federated Learning for Robust Privacy-Preserving Medical Diagnosis Across Hospitals

An experimental framework for evaluating federated medical image classification under cross-hospital domain shift.

FEDERATED LEARNING

Collaborative training across hospitals 0, 1, and 2 without centralizing raw images.

PRIVACY

Differentially private local optimization in DP-WHFedDG with gradient clipping and noise.

DOMAIN GENERALIZATION

Evaluation on completely unseen hospital 4 node (80,974 patches, 9 patients).

What This Research Achieved

Key methodological milestones and empirical findings.

01

UNSEEN-HOSPITAL EVALUATION

The experiment does not evaluate only on the same domains used for training. Training uses Hospitals 0, 1, and 2; validation uses Hospital 3; and the final test set is strictly Hospital 4 (80,974 test samples, 9 patients). This provides a rigorous test of cross-hospital generalization.

02

PATIENT-LEVEL LEAKAGE CONTROL

Patient overlap was explicitly audited and eliminated. The final test set excludes patients 17, 20, 46, and 86. Final validation/test patient overlap is 0, and training/test patient overlap is 0. Patient-level separation prevents patch correlation leakage from artificially inflating test metrics.

03

PRIVACY-ORIENTED FEDERATED LEARNING

DP-WHFedDG incorporates per-sample gradient processing, gradient clipping (norm = 1.0), and Gaussian noise addition (multiplier = 0.8) across 5 federated rounds. Note: Privacy accounting is an approximate analytical RDP estimate and not a certified end-to-end guarantee.

04

MULTIPLE FEDERATED BASELINES

Evaluates standard FedAvg (federated averaging), FedProx (proximal drift penalty), GroupDRO (worst-domain risk optimization), and DP-WHFedDG, compared alongside centralized ERM.

05

DOMAIN STABILITY ANALYSIS

DP-WHFedDG produced the smallest mean absolute validation-to-test performance gap (4.766 pp), demonstrating robust domain stability, though GroupDRO retains higher absolute accuracy.

06

RESEARCH-LEVEL ERROR ANALYSIS

Beyond global accuracy, the study examines precision, recall, F1, AUROC, specificity, FPR, FNR, and complete confusion matrices to characterize model risk profiles.

07

COMPUTATIONAL COST ANALYSIS

Recorded federated round execution times show DP-WHFedDG requires 1,310.38 seconds per round compared to FedAvg's 279.94 seconds due to per-sample gradient ops.

08

HONEST NEGATIVE RESULT

DP-FedAvg was attempted but suffered training instability (exploding loss gradients). Invalid results were discarded and the slot is kept reserved for future extension.

Research Takeaways

"GroupDRO achieved the strongest absolute predictive performance in the completed experiments, while DP-WHFedDG showed the smallest observed validation-to-unseen-test performance gap. These results suggest a trade-off between predictive performance, privacy-oriented optimization, domain stability, and computational cost."

DP-WHFedDG should therefore not be presented as the highest-performing model in this experiment. Its main observed strength is comparatively stable validation-to-unseen-test behavior under the evaluated protocol. DP-FedAvg remains a planned extension for isolating the incremental effect of differential privacy relative to standard FedAvg.

Medical datasets are distributed across institutions due to privacy regulations and data ownership. Federated learning enables collaborative model optimization without centralizing patient images.

Why Differential Privacy?

Local Data ➔ Per-sample Gradients ➔ Gradient Clipping (1.0) ➔ Gaussian Noise (0.8) ➔ Private Update ➔ Federated Aggregation

Federated learning reduces raw data centralization, but model updates can still leak information. Differential privacy adds formal bounds by clipping per-sample gradients and injecting calibrated noise.

Research Limitations

Transparent accounting of study bounds and open challenges.

  1. Single primary random seed: Completed experiments use seed 42. Statistical significance claims require multi-seed trials (e.g. 10 seeds).
  2. Approximate privacy accounting: RDP privacy bounds are analytical estimates rather than formally certified privacy proofs.
  3. DP-FedAvg baseline incomplete: The DP-FedAvg training trial suffered numerical instability and was excluded from final quantitative analysis.
  4. Controlled ablation incomplete: The current study does not isolate privacy, domain weighting, and FL optimization in strict single-factor ablations.
  5. Dataset scope: Evaluation is conducted exclusively on Camelyon17 histopathology patches.
  6. External validity: Additional datasets and hospital centers are required before generalizing clinical claims.
